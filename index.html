Field notes

How does an algorithm learn the unrolled coordinates?

You know how this simulated sheet was rolled. Isomap only sees sampled points. It connects nearby samples, estimates surface distances by following short steps through that graph, and finds coordinates that approximately preserve those distances. Connect too many neighbors and edges can jump between layers.

How does a new data point get an embedding?

Classical manifold-learning methods often assign coordinates directly to the training samples. Placing a new sample can require an extension, such as interpolation from nearby points. A trained autoencoder has an encoder function you can apply directly to a new input.

What do the other methods preserve?

MDS tries to preserve supplied pairwise distances. Kernel PCA finds variance directions after a nonlinear feature mapping. Maximum variance unfolding spreads points apart while holding local distances fixed.

LLE preserves how each point is reconstructed from its neighbors. Laplacian eigenmaps keep strongly connected neighbors close. t-SNE and UMAP emphasize neighborhood relationships and are often used for visualization.

Classical MDS and standard Isomap use dense eigenvalue problems. LLE and Laplacian eigenmaps can exploit sparse neighborhood matrices. t-SNE iteratively optimizes a nonconvex objective, so its layout can depend on initialization.
